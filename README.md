# constellation-site

Static site for [constellation-works.com](https://constellation-works.com) — the front door over
the `constellation-works` projects.

`public/` is the deployed output; Cloudflare Pages serves it verbatim. The one build step
generates the Notes section into `public/notes/`, the Ranking page into `public/ranking/`
and the analytics pages into `public/analytics/` (all ignored by git) before each deploy.

- `public/index.html` — the home page, one file, styles inline.
- `notes/<slug>/index.md` — one note: front matter, Markdown, and the files it links to
  (data, `card.png` for the link preview). `build.py` documents the front matter.
- `build.py` — builds each note page, the `/notes/` index, the Atom feed,
  `/ranking/` from the newest `data/analytics/<YYYY-MM-DD>/scorecards.json` and `rank.json`,
  `/analytics/metrics/` from the newest `data/analytics/<YYYY-MM-DD>/metrics.json`,
  `/analytics/reliability/` from the newest snapshot's `reliability.json`,
  and `/analytics/experiments/` from its `experiments.json`.
  All analytics pages are static and make no Orbit calls.
- `public/assets/` — Notes stylesheet and theme scripts. Tokens mirror `index.html`.
- `public/brand/` — logo assets generated by the brand kit's `build.py`. Do not hand-edit an
  SVG here; regenerate it at the source and re-copy.

## Deploy

Pushes to `main` deploy via [.github/workflows/website.yml](.github/workflows/website.yml).
Requires repository secrets `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, and a
production environment variable `CLOUDFLARE_PAGES_PROJECT` naming the existing Pages project
bound to `constellation-works.com`.

## Notes

A note ships only when its front matter says `status: published` and has a `date`.
Drafts are skipped by the deploy build, so merging a draft does not publish it. Every
note goes through the marketing repo's approval workflow first; flipping a note to
`published` and pushing to `main` is the act of publishing.

Link cards are 1200x630 and generated by the brand kit (`marketing/brand/card.py`).

### Weekly crew-ranking notes

A weekly note is the frozen record of the `/ranking/` crew boards for the 30 days ending
on its date (00:00 UTC). On the Orbit owner host,
`python3 scripts/crew_rankings.py <YYYY-MM-DD>` reads the scorecards with `--until`,
freezes them in `data/crew-rankings/<date>/scorecards.json` (projected as the analytics
snapshot projects them) and writes a draft `notes/crew-rankings-<date>/` with its CSVs.
The note is titled `[Crew Ranking - <date>]: MVP <crew>`; the MVP is the rank-1 row of the
all-tiers implementer board. The MVP line, summary and byline name it with the boards' own
`crew_label` (`haiku@xhigh (claude-haiku-5-5), MVP of the week, 146.2`; an MVP whose effort is
unknown reads `opus · effort unknown (claude-opus-5-5), MVP of the week, 146.2`), so writing a
note needs `markdown` (`uv run --with markdown==3.8.2`); the title and `--writer-only` use
the plain crew, and the crew `--writer-only` prints writes the note.
Its body's `<!-- crews-by-role: data/crew-rankings/<date> -->` marker is replaced at build
time by the Ranking page's own `render_crews_by_role`, every role stacked. The MVP crew
replaces the `{{victory-address}}` placeholder; `build.py` refuses a published note that
still has it. The method is [notes/crew-rankings-method](notes/crew-rankings-method/index.md).

## Ranking

`/ranking/` is a top-level page (nav: Notes · Ranking · Analytics · Orbit · GitHub)
generated by `build.py` from the newest snapshot only, its 30-day window: no window
chips, no earlier-snapshot links and no monthly archive. It replaced
`/analytics/agents/`; `public/_redirects` sends `/analytics/agents/` and every
`/analytics/agents/<YYYY-MM>/` to `/ranking/` with a 301. Four tabs, each opening
with four stat cards:

- **Overall** — a podium and two labelled boards, two methodologies. First **Model
  rank** ("who implements best"): the snapshot's `rank.json`, Orbit's live 30-day
  managed model rank (`GET /api/agents/rank?window=30d&execution=managed&live=true`),
  band-grouped rows with p10–p90 whiskers on a shared axis, $ per task and cost
  ratio, tier mix, landed %, an expandable per-model breakdown, one line saying the
  board is live and provisional, and a folded "Not ranked yet" list whose rule and
  per-model reasons are Orbit's text from `rank.json`. The podium is its top three
  in Rank's order. Then the **All-roles index** ("who contributes most across
  implementing, reviewing and piloting"): a model's role index is its role score
  divided by that role's fleet average × 100, crews pooled into their model by
  units, weighed 60/25/15 over the roles the model holds with the weights rescaled
  to sum to 1. A model needs a ranked crew in at least two roles; one-role models
  are listed, not ranked, and no missing role is imputed. The weights and the
  minimum live in one constant, `OVERALL_RULE` in `build.py`, and the page states
  the rule from it. A snapshot without `rank.json` shows one line in place of
  Model rank and the podium follows the All-roles index, saying so; a `rank.json`
  from before the live board (settled) still renders, labelled settled.
  `scripts/analytics_snapshot.py` writes `rank.json` under the snapshot's `rank`
  key: live and provisional, managed and 30-day only, with the exact window,
  Orbit's eligibility rule as one sentence (`eligibility_rule`) and each
  non-eligible model's reason, free text sanitized, gap models and crews dropped
  and counted, and paired
  probabilities and per-effort splits left out. The crew `system` is not a
  configured crew (it is Orbit's attribution for system-run work), so the
  exporter drops it from those crew lists and counts it with the other dropped
  gap crews, and the page never shows it. A bare crew key carries no recorded
  effort, so it is labelled `crew · effort unknown`, the label the crew boards
  use. No effort is read from crew configuration, which changes over time, so
  nothing on the site guesses an effort.
- **Implementers** — "Crews by role":
  takeaway callouts, the crew table with the fleet-average marker, and implementers
  by tier. Crews by role is one renderer, `render_crews_by_role(directory, role=None)`,
  which takes a snapshot directory so a weekly note can reuse it. It never draws a role
  toggle: with a role it renders that role's boards (each /ranking/ role tab), and
  without one it stacks every role under a plain heading (the weekly note's body). A scorecard row is one
  crew, model and recorded effort, so every crew board names the crew as `crew@effort`
  (`sol@high`; a crew already named `haiku@high` stays as is). A row whose effort was not
  recorded reads `sol · effort unknown`, with no estimate and no note, in every snapshot,
  older ones included. Snapshots from before the effort split show bare crew names.
- **Reviewers** — the provisional/settled toggle, each board with its own window.
- **Pilots** — the pilot crew table.

Every board states its score credits and debits once. "Method and windows" sits
below the tabs on every tab, linking the
[crew-rankings note](notes/crew-rankings-method/index.md), the weekly write-ups
(published notes tagged `rankings` and `weekly`) and the snapshot JSON. Without
JavaScript the tabs render as stacked panels and every breakdown is a native
`<details>`. The page is rebuilt on each deploy and is not committed;
`public/ranking/` is gitignored, the same way `public/notes/` is.

## Analytics

`/analytics/metrics/` is generated by `analytics_page.py` (called from `build.py`) from the
newest `data/analytics/<YYYY-MM-DD>/metrics.json`. It shows the succeeded-run 30-day duration
board alongside 30-day boards for task completions, defects, tokens, cost, diff size, friction
and turns. The page states its UTC window once at the top; a board prints its own window only
when it differs. An at-a-glance tile row (blue improved, orange got worse, per each metric's
direction; `*` marks a rate derived on the page) links into four groups: Speed, Throughput,
Quality and Spend. Coverage and thin-sample warnings are computed from the snapshot. Daily
tables and per-model rows sit in `<details>`. The outcome-by-complexity board is an all-time
mix with no 30-day window. Charts are inline SVG generated at build time; the page makes no
Orbit calls. `public/assets/analytics.js` turns the groups into tabs; without JavaScript every
group renders stacked.

`/analytics/reliability/` uses the newest snapshot's `reliability.json`. The headline is
the settled success rate (successes plus direct failures as the denominator; cascaded
wrapper echoes are excluded), with tiles for recovery (`totals.recovery.per_job_run`),
cascaded echoes and other outcomes. One stacked bar shows where every recorded run
ended, with every outcome count in a disclosure. A daily failure-rate chart comes from
`over_time`: rates recomputed from counts, partial first and last days faded, a
30-day reference line, a settled-runs strip, labelled spikes, and per-day text and a
table fallback. `Failures by job` breaks the settled rate down per `by_job[].job_id`,
sortable by count or rate (progressive enhancement); the snapshot's `low_sample` flag
marks small samples, which sink when sorted by rate, and zero-failure jobs fold into
one disclosure. Only job ids matching the `SAFE_JOB_ID` allowlist reach the page;
each shows its `JOB_LABELS` display name (for example, `orb20863_visual_eval` maps to
`Visual eval`), falling back to the raw id. The renderer reads only known aggregate
counts, allowlisted job ids and validated timestamps; it does not publish activity
identifiers, diagnostic labels or messages.

`/analytics/experiments/` includes only concluded experiments from the newest snapshot.
Picker cards (status, short question, arms, tasks, days, clear-better and clear-worse
counts) lead to one section per experiment: a readout strip (verdict, chosen arm, primary
metric, enrolled), the question (full text in `<details>`), why no decision was made, the
clear signals (cells whose interval excludes zero) and one metric × arm scorecard of
differences from control, primary row first. Each cell shows the value, a Better / Worse /
Leans / Same tag, an interval plot oriented worse-left/better-right by the metric's
direction, and the interval; only clear cells are shaded. Metrics unmeasured for every arm
fold into one line, exploratory metrics sit under `Other metrics (N)`, and an arms table
shows treatment delivery (tasks on the arm's own crew) and observed crews. The short
question is assembled from the readout's arms, primary metric, population and guardrails.
`analytics.js` shows one experiment at a time and adds a "Clear signals only" filter;
without JavaScript every experiment renders stacked. Per-arm means for every
outcome, medians for non-rate outcomes, supplied intervals and metric-specific
support appear only inside the collapsed `Raw per-arm outcomes` block. It prints the
cumulative UTC window from experiment start to readout. The statistical verdict
and recorded chosen arm are distinct: concluding an experiment does not imply a
winner, and unavailable measurements stay unavailable.
Names, hypotheses and verdict reasons use the snapshot's sanitized public text;
individual task/run records and diagnostic fields are not rendered.

Each analytics page (metrics, reliability and experiments; not Ranking) also has a monthly archive: once a calendar month has
ended (a snapshot from a later month exists), that month's last snapshot is
rendered at `/analytics/<page>/<YYYY-MM>/` through the same renderer as the
live page, starting with 2026-11; nothing before the first routine snapshot
(2026-11-08) is backfilled. Every live and archived page carries a snapshot
picker (a plain link list, no JavaScript) listing Latest and each archived
month, and the `/analytics/` index lists the available months.
